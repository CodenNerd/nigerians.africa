The problem

02

People cannot see the public life that shapes them.

Rights, services, and accountability depend on information people understand and trust. In Nigeria that information is scattered across gazettes, budgets, ministry sites, news, and rumour.

01Who is responsible?

A stalled road, a dark street, a clinic that never opened — which office owns it.

02Where did the money go?

What was allocated, what was released, and what was reported as spent.

03What actually happened?

The promise, the project status, the evidence, and the official reply, in one place.

04What can I do next?

Who to contact, what to keep, and how to make a report or a request.

Why it stays broken

03

The pieces are published. The thread is missing.

A budget names a figure.

Allocations sit in documents that few people can search, and fewer can connect to a place they know.

A signboard names a contractor.

A project appears, stalls, and leaves. The office, the payment, and the outcome drift apart.

A report names a delay.

News and social posts move on. The evidence, the response, and the history do not stay attached.

People are asked to hold institutions accountable with fragments.

The solution

05

A public memory of civic life.

NigeriaForNigerians connects government, places, people, problems, projects, money, evidence, and history as one graph. Finding a fact is the start of engaging the institution behind it.

01DiscoverSearch a place, a project, or a question.
02UnderstandSee the office, the money, and the status.
03VerifyCheck the source, the date, and the label.
04ActReport, request, or contact.
05TrackWatch the response arrive on the same page.
06RememberThe outcome stays after the news moves on.

Primary track: Transparency & Accountability. Reporting and protection use the same path, so a record of spending can also become a record of harm and a route to support.

Clear next steps

07

Seeing the record is the beginning.

The prototype turns a fact into a path: who to contact, what to keep, and where a case can go.

Guidance

What should I do?

Plain language on rights, evidence to keep, the responsible office, and where to report — from a stalled project to a police encounter.

Freedom of Information

Trust and accuracy

08

Every serious claim carries a trail.

Status is on the page.

Official record, reported, under review, disputed, AI Generated. A reader can see what kind of statement they are looking at.

Sources are required.

Money, claims, office-holder history, and evidence are refused when a source link is missing. The check runs before the record updates.

A person reviews first.

Public data lands in a staging area. A reviewer approves it before it enters the record. The crawler never writes the live record directly.

AI explains. It cites.

Ask retrieves from the graph, then summarises. Thin evidence is described as thin. The model does not invent a source or a verdict.

Sources in this proof of concept: Wikidata, Wikimedia Commons, the Budget Office of the Federation, curated government pages, and citizen submissions. Each carries a publisher and a link.

Real conditions

09

Useful where the connection is thin and the stakes are high.

Trust

Who said it, when, what supports it, and whether the status later changed. History stays visible.

Low bandwidth

Maps have list fallbacks. The record is text, status, and links, readable when images are too heavy.

Inclusion

Plain-language guidance, semantic pages, a skip link, and status a screen reader can name.

Privacy

Reading is open. Submitting requires sign-in. Guidance says to capture only what is lawful and safe.

Languages

English in this prototype. Translations can sit beside the graph — Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, Pidgin — without rewriting relationships.

Local reality

States, local governments, wards, offices, and laws are specific. Another community reuses the model with its own institutions.

Scale

10

Scale is another place on the same graph.

Nigeria is already the map.

States, local governments, and wards are first-class places. A new community is a new location with its offices, problems, and projects attached.

Office, money, project, evidence, response. Institutions and laws change. The path from a problem to a next step stays the same.

Cross-track by design: bribery guidance, election observation, and the Make Nigeria Better scheme sit on the accountability record, so reporting leads somewhere.

AI coding

11

Built with AI coding tools. Grounded in the public record.

Inside the product

  • Ask retrieves matching records from the graph before any summary is written.
  • Answers are labelled AI Generated and cite the pages they used.
  • With no model key, the same question still returns the structured record.
  • The model is instructed to point back to citations and to avoid verdicts.

How the prototype was made

  • The civic idea — a public memory people can follow and act on — was set by the team.
  • AI coding tools in Cursor implemented the domain graph, the public interface, ingestion, and the connected seed.
  • Judges can run the journey locally: install, start, and open the Allen Avenue thread.
  • The proof of concept is the argument: every documented path is navigable.
